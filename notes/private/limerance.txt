foreword:
humans are flawed. so am I.
at times, the rational brain simply shuts down.
this is perhaps one of those non-deterministic, irrational moments.

winter brings with it a certain melancholic charm

it seems to whisper the word impermanence.

but that word, that exact word,
has a paradoxical sibling.

every new beginning carries the seed of an ending,
and every ending makes room for a new beginning.

that's the logic of autumn
not denial of impermanence,
but the act of defying despair through renewal.

"yes, everything ends... and still, I'll begin again."

as the year closes, it feels like a quiet subtraction
the world rearranging itself
while you're still trying to make sense of your own coordinates.

and somewhere inside that subtraction
lies an unexpected addition
a quiet, almost invisible rebellion against entropy itself.

impermanence as promise:
people change, people move away,
sometimes they even leave for good.

but there's a strange thrill in choosing to participate in time,
instead of hiding from it.

something might not be immortal because it lasts forever;
it's immortal because it changes you before it ends.

something that doesn't cancel impermanence,
but illuminates it.

it's the cherry blossom's lesson made human
you cherish it because it will fall.

without impermanence, there is no urgency,
no pulse, no tenderness.

the sweetness of a new connection
depends on knowing it could vanish.
the heart knows this instinctively
that's why beginnings feel both ecstatic and fragile.

the beauty of life isn't that it lasts,
it's that it happens at all.

the leaves fall, yes
but in spring, something blooms
that couldn't have existed without that fall.

yes, everything ends.
and still, here you are beginning again.

maybe that's what draws us to create,
to write, to think, to reach across time,
even when we know the moment won't last.

something keeps us trying anyway,
something that exists beyond logic,
beyond permanence,
beyond reason itself.

something that makes you stay up late chasing meaning,
that makes you look twice at the ordinary,
that turns coincidence into a constellation.

call it what you will.
but what is that something?

on the unspoken:

so here's where words meet their limit
some things resist direct translation
they live in subtext, in tone, in the space between lines
i wonder if you can read the shape of what i'm not quite saying,

if the outline is clear enough
even when the center says unnamed
or maybe i'm still speaking in riddles
when i'm trying to be honest

i suppose that i'm asking is:
does this land anywhere near where you're standing?
can you see what i'm tracing around
or am i jsu drawing circles in my own head?

...

i'm leaving this open ended on purpose
not because i'm afraid of clarity
but because some things need room to breathe
before they become declaration
so tell me, did any of this translate?
or am i just thinking out loud



in a language, only i speak?

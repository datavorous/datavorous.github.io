[25/06, 8:22 am] sagnik bhattacharjee: speed learning in my opinion 

As for me, my favourite tool in my skillbox is asking questions. It's a 4 layered process.

1. first layer is the inventory layer 
- "what is even happening here?"
- list down every possible axis the problem space depends on
- you are not going to jump directly into finding a solution, you will just map the problem space 

2. second layer is to find the levers
- "what are the things affecting our problem?"
- list them down, keep "formulation" as a premium lever

3. third layer is to prioritize
- "which of these is a whale?" 
- find a way to rank analytically when you cant have them measured 

4. fourth layer is to find constraints
- "what am i not allowed to do?"
- "what does moving my current lever cause to other factors?"

Measure, repeat until you find the right combination of the levers.

This is not immune to all types of problems. When the fog is epistemic (you don't know what you don't know) then it fails. 

Try out with any problem, use any AI for your aid.

[25/06, 8:29 am] sagnik bhattacharjee: Karpathy's autoresearch or Claude Code's /goal, they operate in the first 2 zones. 

What's transformational then? 
Let me give an example. Now, there's a field called RL (reinforcement learning). Since 2021, the field has had very less progress. Why? Researchers were opting towards sample efficiency (how much progress you can make per unit data), and in turn made the compute efficiency (how much progress you can make per unit compute time) slower. That made experiments take WEEKs.

Joseph Suarez in 2023 came up with PufferLib, his thesis is that, FLOPs are cheap, so we brute force our way into progress in RL, by keeping Compute Efficiency in the x-axis of the problem.

p.s. Atari took 2 weeks to be solved using prior methods(viz. DeepMind's paper on '3 dark triads'), PufferLib bruteforces into doing the same within a minute 

This is transformational, you _shift_ the framing of the problem.

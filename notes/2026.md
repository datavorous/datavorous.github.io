This note is titled 2026.txt, but I have been meaning to put these thoughts down since I was 15, which was almost 5 years ago. We are witnessing a widespread collective cognitive decline, and I believe this would be one of the very few artifacts on the internet untainted by AI -- this is an attempt to capture a snapshot of the current state of reality, through the flavour of my own unique consciousness. I often write down my stream of thoughts; there are no climaxes and conclusions, there is no narrator or a main character. Just time ~ thoughts, co-existing. 

I have had various _special_ interests in my life, and one of the longest standing ones is the meta-analysis of human behaviour (especially as a grand collective). I have encountered hundreds of people in my life, some absurdly intelligent, some questionably dumb. And one of the most recurring trait I have noticed is the abrasive tendency to avoid anything "meta". "It is not that deep" is _the_ most infuriating combination of words that has ever been spoken to me, and now when I reflect, it does make sense. The average Joe or Jane is capable of deep thoughts, but most people are somehow conditioned over the years to avoid them, why? It's mentally taxing and has no immediate payoff, even at a life-horizon scale. Devoting time to the abstract, mysterious and seemingly obvious things does not make sense from a natural point of view. All of these come with a certain level of anxiety, because all of it is uncertain. Unpredictability breeds anxiety. Anxiety is not a natural state of any biological being. We would rather prefer to take refuge in this mundane world of ours, despite living in a world full of spectacle and drama. The true significance of human life, or the lack there of, is not really a concern. The average person like you & me, would easily lead to the concretization of terror management theory. Detaching our biological safeguard won't make sense when we can distract ourselves with work, religion, sex, drugs etc. 

But has reality diverged? Ever since we ("humans") developed language, the perception of reality synced higher with our consciousness. This is the input phase. Information as we know it, would not arrive to you in the same form as it happened. There are master-observers, they interpret the reality, and what we know about the world is essentially a fragment of their consciousness copied into yours. That's _nearly_ everything -- except the things you truly observe. We are not questioning an individual's way of perceiving reality, or whether something truly is what it is. Instead, I wish to focus on the mechanical propagation of it. In a way, we all are part of a collective hive of intelligence. We are `mmap()`-ed into each other's interpretations. Interpretations depend upon the language you think in. When I do not have a word for a feeling or a pattern, I can not make sense of it in my brain. It feels like an untouchable slimy coloured blob which I can't quite see through. It's a paradox. Language does not dictate reality, but it _does_ dictate which parts of reality become available to consciousness. So if you think about it, what if a master-observer (spawned randomly) missed out something due to the weakness of their language set? As they become the new propagator of information, the receives nodes ("us", "humans") never get to know what something truly was. In cognitive literature, it resonates with the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis. You can not optimize for something which you can not distinguish. Last year, nearly around this time (autumn of 2025), I wanted to express a certain variant of nostalgia, which trigged after I studied about liminal spaces. English does not have a word for it. Neither did Bengali. When I wanted to explain liminal spaces to somebody who had never heard of it before, I could not make them understand what it truly means. That's what I wanted to explain. This is one such instance. Billions of humans, over thousand of years, had trillion-trillions such interactions, which means, our collected consciousness is fundamentally unaware of everything around us. One human suddenly can not come into existence, and solve everything. One might argue that I am pointing towards linguistic elitism, which I thought at first too. But then I realised that it is all about _granularity_ of concepts. An infant can feel before it learns to speak, right? Then perhaps we do not need words to feel something, but maybe how we _reflect_ does. We can not think recursively without a syntax which allows for embedded clauses, similarly formal logic without "if"s and "therefores"s is impossible. I am hinting towards Chomsky hierarchy now, but I think it is not the right place for that. In simple words: certain cultures intuitively understood _nothingness_, but only did a few of them unlock a new area of reasoning when the concept/word for "zero" came into existence. We just need these to improve our ability to manipulate our understanding in a structured way. Language shapes the boundaries of what can be explicitly reasoned, nested and compared around. I can express "the nostalgia for a time you never lived" in English, but _mono no aware_ gives me better portability and the ability to manipulate _something_ in my cognitive space. My instrument is incomplete, so are yours, there are things which we can't operate on, _yet_ or _never_? ~~I do not know~~ WE do not know. The loss (over time) is done, will be done, let us accept this.


> There are more things in heaven and earth, Horatio, Than are dreamt of in your philosophy. 
- Hamlet (1.5.167-8), Hamlet to Horatio

The emphasis here should be on "dreamt of", as Hamlet is pointing out how little even the most educated people can explain. 

I was supposed to write down the mechanics of human thought propagation, let me get back there. I started reading Encyclopedia Britannica in 5th grade, I read 2 volumes of it (retention % of the exact facts is not our concern), and the most striking realization was that everything can be modelled as a system. Indus civilization, WWII, black plague, emergence of the motor vehicle -- everything. All of it points back to how we handle scarcity and coordination, information and the control loops of which, and the most important: power asymmetry in the system. Post-modern world, as we live it, is no different. But does something feel off? The system is pretty much the same for the last 20 to 30 years, trends come and go but are constrained the aforementioned axes all the time. But DOES something feel off? Like a collective shift in our consciousness? Maybe, a state of a subconscious sensory perception that something is going to happen, just like animals and birds? 

This is a theory ("conspiracy"?) I have been developing post-2022. _Something_ accelerated. _The_ pandemic was a catalyst. Billions of people were affected at the same period of time. Almost everyone had a stable view of the world around us, and suddenly things flipped. The realization, the glimpse of how the world can be a different place than what it looks like every day or at least the stories that we tell to ourselves, the dreams that have been injected into our minds, that don't have roots in us but rather we do it anyway for the survival. They are all... bleak. Nothing matters as much any more as it did before. Everything has a certain sense of dullness to it. Be it festivals, starting of a new school year, birthdays etc. We realised that we could live without a lot of things we found traditionally "important" in society. Is this a mass psychological response to a collective trauma? Government bodies showing that they do not care about the people as much as they say? That the events and necessities in your life, they are as bleak as a sheet of glass? Or is because we saw humans dying like insects, the same ones who dreamt of travelling the world 7 months back, or was about to graduate as an engineer in 2 years -- but never did? Or was it the forced addition and dependence on technology, which opened up a new way for us to interact with reality, as our _primary_ means? 

Billions of people simultaneously realised the fragility of institutions (schools, government) and the arbitrariness of traditional life milestones (career goals). This forced reliance on tech as our primary lens for reality, paired with witnessing life rendered cheap and fragile, resulted in a widespread psychological numbness -- a collective shift where traditional sources of meaning lost their resonance, completely. For a farmer in the Middle East, the halt of global supply chains affected him, and continue to do so. For a middle schooler in India, she watched her physical transition into adolescence dissolve into a parasocial dependence on technology. The family man in the US discovered that his corporate labour, the core of his socially prescribed identity, can be paused, outsourced or erased. 

All these people, despite operating within very different socio-economic control loops, they all got to look behind the curtain of the fragile mechanism. They-we-collectively realised that our inherited life trajectories are not laws of physics, but highly contingent, arbitrary agreements. When those agreements failed globally, the resulting cognitive dissonance has hardened/is hardening into a universal numbness. We are now billions of disjointed nodes, now aware of the simulation's fragility, yet we continue to operate within it. Purely out of biological momentum.

Every year since 2023, I watch 4 episodes of Black Mirror -- Bandersnatch and Smithereens make total sense now, right? 

Let me summarise whatever I had spilled over in the last 1600+ words. In short, reality exists, but our access to it is throttled by the bandwidth of our language. So we build fragile systems to manage our terror of the unknown, but when a global catalyst shatters those systems, the illusion dies. As we lack the language to construct a new reality, we default to running the old code without believing in it. What happens when we do something without believing in it? Dissatisfaction.

As of 2026, we have the highest amount of alive people in history, at once, on Earth (~ISS is an anomaly). 7% of all of human population is alive right now, that means we have the highest raw collective consciousness operating, ever. How cool _is_ that! And still, we are stressed out. Let me lay down another thread in which I talk what is happening underneath this vast system.

As the inherited structures around us have lost some of their legitimacy, then love changes too. Relationships become harder to separate from markets, algorithms, visibility, optionality and performance. Work becomes harder to believe in when everyone can see how arbitrary much of it is, and now AI is making even competence feel temporary. Anxiety stops being a response to one particular threat and becomes environmental: there is always another model, another war, another layoff, another crisis, another indication that the future is arriving faster than our ability to form a stable picture of it.

People still survive, fall in love, they still study for exams, go to work, get married, cook food, remember the lost family members and plan trips six months into the future. The machinery STILL continues. But underneath it, what exactly are we building towards? For most of modern history, _even_ miserable people were offered some forward narrative. Inside that sequence, used to be an implicit promise that life would eventually begin to make sense. That promise feels weaker now. Something is attacking the assumption that effort accumulates into permanence. You can spend ten years becoming exceptionally good at something and still wonder whether the _economic value_ of that ability will survive another five. The future no longer appears stable enough to justify long term attachment to any identity. 

And humans need attachment -- people, places, rituals, and to imagined futures. With so much metaphysical weight put on us (referring to young adults now), how will the future stand? Blame AI, blame the decline job market, come up with the theories that pandemic was a long shot project by the people on Epstein files, the naked exposure of corporate greed, be concerned about the rise of a thought-less generation who have lost the sense of reality, the leakage of control loops over everything and what not. Maybe we won't face a dystopia, in a non-binary way, but instead the total descend into societal madness gradually over time. Humanity has never uniformly done what's right, and I challenge you to offer a time when they did. The parallel worlds of our constructed reality and one which it truly is, has collided. Fallout is happening.

Maybe what looks like a crisis of motivation is partly a crisis of believable futures. 

Together, we have all lost the plot.

Hopium is running out, we need something different.
